If fire, theft, or a water leak hit tomorrow, could you prove what you owned and what it was worth? This guide is a practical way to document coins, trading cards, stamps, paper money, lighters, and similar pieces before you need a claim. No bank login is required for the habits below.

Does standard homeowner’s insurance cover a rare collection?

Often only partly. Many policies treat collectibles as unscheduled personal property with a low sublimit — frequently far below what a focused collection is worth. The fix is usually a scheduled rider or a specialty collectibles policy, and insurers expect documentation before they underwrite or pay.

Ask your insurer these three questions

  • What is the unscheduled valuables limit on my policy today?
  • Do you want a scheduled list, a rider, or a separate collectibles policy for this category?
  • What photos, appraisals, or inventory formats do you accept after a loss?

Write the answers down. The rest of this article is how to build the packet they will ask for.

What belongs in an insurance-ready archive?

Three pillars show up again and again.

1. Photos that prove the piece and its condition

For higher-value items, aim for:

  • A clear overall shot of the front (and back when it matters).
  • A closer shot of unique marks: mint mark, serial, grading-label barcode, certificate number, or engraving.
  • Consistent light so condition stays readable years later.

In Curator’s Vault, photos live with each vault item. The insurance PDF can include those photos when you export.

2. Values based on evidence, not wishful asking prices

An active “Buy It Now” price is what someone hopes to get. Prefer purchase price plus a dated estimated value you can explain (sold comps, a guide, a dealer quote). Keep emotional attachment out of the ledger.

3. A copy that survives the disaster

A phone-only camera roll in the same house as the collection is a single point of failure. Export or back up so you can still open the inventory from somewhere else after a loss.

Why collectors stall — and what to do instead

Macro photos, sold-price research, and off-site copies take time. People start a sheet, add twenty rows, and stop. A workable habit: photograph and add pieces when you acquire them, refresh estimated values on a schedule you will keep, and export an insurance PDF (or backup) after big additions — then store that file off the phone.

When should I pay for a professional appraisal?

Pay when the collection is large, includes suspected rarities, or the insurer / estate process requires a credentialed valuation. For a modest, well-photographed set with purchase records and dated estimates, many people start with a self-built inventory (spreadsheet or vault + Insurance Report) and ask the agent what else they need.

FAQ

Will photos only on my phone be enough after a fire?

Only if a copy still exists somewhere else. Export the Insurance Report PDF or a backup file to a drive or cloud account you can reach after a loss.

Can I use eBay asking prices as proof of value?

Weak evidence. Prefer purchase price, completed sold results, or guides — and record the date and source next to your estimate. The vault’s insurance PDF uses the estimated values you store on each item.

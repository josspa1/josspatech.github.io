The handoff problem

You ask your client for "the last 12 months of transactions, categorized." What you usually get: 12 PDF statements (one per month, no categories), a CSV that stops 90 days back, or worse — a forwarded email with screenshots. PocketBudJet sits between the bank and you.

What banks make your client send you

  • One PDF per statement period — you assemble the year
  • CSV exports limited to 90 days — or 18 months on the ones with newer interfaces
  • No category tagging — just merchant strings and dates
  • Different formats per bank, per account, per credit card
  • Scanned check images instead of structured data
  • Whatever your bookkeeping tool's CSV importer demands — on day three of tax week

What your PocketBudJet client sends you

  • One file. Whole year (or longer). Everything.
  • Pick the format your software accepts — OFX, Excel, PDF, HTML, Word, or "Accountant" layout
  • Categorized to match your chart of accounts naming
  • Schedule A, B, C, D, or E PDFs already laid out by IRS line numbers
  • Clean income/expense splits, deductions itemized, transfer side eliminated
  • Encrypted at rest on the device, encrypted in transit when shared

How the handoff works

Three steps for the client. Zero work for you on the receiving end.

1

Client opens their bank app

They tap Share on a statement, pick PocketBudJet from the share sheet. PocketBudJet pulls the transactions, AI-categorizes them, and stores everything on the device. They never have to hand the bank a third-party login.

2

Client opens Reports → Export

They pick what you need: tax year, period range, category set, or one of the IRS-line-aligned schedule reports. Everything's already organized — they don't have to re-classify anything.

3

Client picks the format you asked for

OFX for desktop tax software. Excel for your own pivot work. PDF for an audit trail. HTML if they want to email a link. Word for a document review. They send you exactly what you need to feed into your tools.

Why your client picked PocketBudJet

PocketBudJet doesn't aggregate your client's bank credentials, doesn't sell their transaction data, and doesn't require them to create an account. Their data stays on the device. Sharing with you is a deliberate, one-time act.

NO BANK LOGIN

PocketBudJet never asks for your client's banking credentials. Statements come in via the OS share sheet, file picker, or camera scan. Their relationship with their bank is unchanged.

DEVICE-FIRST STORAGE

Transactions live in PocketBudJet's private app storage on their phone. Backups are optional, opt-in, and AES-256 encrypted with a key only your client holds.

SHARING IS EXPLICIT

When your client emails you a packet, they pick the file, the format, and the period. PocketBudJet does not auto-share, sync, or surface anything to you they didn't choose to send.

AUDIT-FRIENDLY

Every export includes the source statement file's parse confidence score, import batch ID, and transaction provenance. You can verify which statements rolled into which line items.

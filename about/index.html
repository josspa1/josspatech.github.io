About

Joe, founder of
JosspaTech

Useful tools. You stay in control of your data.

Most budget apps want your bank login. Most collection apps want your data living in their cloud. I didn’t think that trade was fair — so I built something different.

I’m Joe, founder of JosspaTech (pronounced Joss-pah-tech).

It started with my son. I wanted him to have a clear, honest budget tool — something that actually helped, not something that asked for his bank password. Then I realized other people needed the same thing. Not just tracking spending — finding ways to keep more of their money. And getting transactions into the app without handing a third party their login. Those companies may be secure, but when you hold thousands of people’s banking information, you’ve also made yourself a target.

So I found another way: you import your own statements. Your data stays on your phone. That’s PocketBudJet.

Handy Horology Helper came from a close friend — a jeweler and vintage watch dealer. He knew watches and clocks cold, and he still kept a library of reference books. I always wished software could make that research easier. After he passed, I kept thinking about collectors who still need that kind of help. So HHH can take what you know — or just a photo — and fill in the blanks… and let you catalog your collection.

Then Curator’s Vault, for coins, paper money, stamps, and more. And Unscribe, for people who mainly need to manage subscriptions — same idea: no bank login required, your information stays yours.

Across every app, the constant is the same: useful tools, and you stay in control of your data.
